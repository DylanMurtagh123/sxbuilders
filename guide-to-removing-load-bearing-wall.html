Removing a Load-Bearing Wall: Permissions, RSJs and the Work Involved

Removing a supporting wall can transform a cramped ground floor, but it is structural work rather than a simple strip-out. Proper assessment, calculations, temporary support, steelwork and building-control inspection protect the property and create a compliant opening.

Quick answer

Removing a supporting wall can transform a cramped ground floor, but it is structural work rather than a simple strip-out. Proper assessment, calculations, temporary support, steelwork and building-control inspection protect the property and create a compliant opening.

For property-specific advice, speak to the appropriate planning, design or building-control professional and request a clear written scope from your builder.

How do you know whether a wall is load bearing?

Its position and direction can provide clues, but appearance alone is not reliable. A wall may support floors, roof members, another wall or a chimney above. Construction drawings, inspection of the floors and roof, and professional assessment may be needed.

Never begin demolition to “find out”. The load path and support method should be understood before the wall is disturbed.

Structural engineer, steel beam and building control

A structural engineer can calculate the beam, bearings, padstones, posts or other support required. Although “RSJ” is commonly used as a general term, the specified member may be another type of steel section.

Structural alterations normally need building-regulations approval. Building control may inspect temporary arrangements, bearings, fire protection and the completed work against the approved design.

What happens during the work?

  1. Confirm the intended opening and structural design.
  2. Protect the work area and identify services running through the wall.
  3. Install the designed temporary support.
  4. Form the opening carefully and prepare bearings.
  5. Install the specified beam and supporting details.
  6. Complete fire protection and building-control inspections.
  7. Make good plaster, floors, ceilings, electrics and joinery.

Plan the finished room as well as the opening

Wall removal often forms part of a kitchen or wider ground-floor project. Radiators, switches, sockets, flooring transitions, ceiling levels and kitchen positions should be planned before work begins.

Frequently asked questions

Do I always need an RSJ?

Not every opening uses the same support. The structural design determines whether a steel beam, lintel, timber member or another solution is appropriate.

Do I need planning permission?

Internal wall removal often does not need planning permission, but listed buildings and some special circumstances differ. Building regulations commonly apply.

Can pipes and cables be inside the wall?

Yes. Electrical, plumbing, heating or other services may need to be isolated and rerouted.

How long does wall removal take?

The structural opening may be formed relatively quickly, but preparation, inspections, fire protection and making good add to the programme.

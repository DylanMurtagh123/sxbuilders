Whole-House Renovation Checklist: What to Plan Before Work Starts

Quick answer

A whole-house renovation succeeds when decisions are made in the right order. Structural work and hidden services come before finishes; long-lead products need choosing early; and the quote must define which rooms, repairs and final details are included.

For property-specific advice, speak to the appropriate planning, design or building-control professional and request a clear written scope from your builder.

1. Understand the property

  • Record known damp, movement, roof, drainage or service issues.
  • Consider specialist surveys where the age or condition warrants them.
  • Check planning constraints and previous alterations.
  • Identify elements being retained and protected.
  • Confirm access, parking, storage and waste arrangements.

2. Define scope room by room

Write down work for every room, hallway and external interface. Include demolition, structure, windows, doors, heating, electrics, plumbing, plaster, joinery, flooring and decoration. This reduces assumptions and makes quotations easier to compare.

Decide whether the project is best described as renovation or refurbishment using our guide to renovation versus refurbishment.

3. Put the work in a sensible sequence

  1. Surveys, design and approvals
  2. Strip-out and investigation
  3. Structural work and weatherproofing
  4. First-fix plumbing, heating and electrics
  5. Insulation, plastering and drying
  6. Second-fix joinery, services, kitchens and bathrooms
  7. Flooring, decoration, testing and handover

The actual sequence should be tailored to the property and agreed scope.

4. Prepare the household and budget

Decide whether the house can remain occupied and which services may be unavailable. Protect important belongings and plan for pets, children and remote working. Read our guide to living at home during renovation.

Keep an appropriate contingency and a decision schedule for products such as windows, sanitaryware, tiles, flooring and kitchens. Late selections can affect both programme and cost.

Frequently asked questions

Should I renovate one room at a time?

That can reduce the immediate scope, but repeated setup and changes to shared services may be less efficient. The right approach depends on budget and occupancy.

What should be chosen before work begins?

Layouts, major finishes and long-lead items should be resolved early enough to support pricing and sequencing.

Do I need to move out?

Not always, but extensive structural work or loss of essential services can make temporary accommodation safer and more practical.

How should changes be managed?

Record the changed scope, price and programme effect in writing before the additional work proceeds.
